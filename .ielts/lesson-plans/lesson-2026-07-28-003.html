The Great Barrier Reef

The Great Barrier Reef, located off the coast of Queensland, Australia, is the world's largest coral reef system. It stretches over 2,300 kilometers and covers an area of approximately 344,400 square kilometers. The reef was first explored by European navigator Captain James Cook in 1770 when his ship, the HMS Endeavour, ran aground on the reef. However, indigenous Australian peoples had been living along the coast and using the reef's resources for at least 40,000 years before European arrival.

The reef is home to an extraordinary diversity of marine life, including more than 1,500 species of fish, 400 types of coral, and 30 species of whales and dolphins. In 1981, UNESCO designated the Great Barrier Reef as a World Heritage Site. Despite this protection, the reef faces serious threats. Between 2016 and 2020, the reef experienced three major bleaching events caused by rising ocean temperatures. A report by the Australian Institute of Marine Science in 2020 found that approximately 30% of the reef's coral had died due to bleaching. In 2019, the Australian government allocated approximately 2 billion Australian dollars for reef conservation and research.
